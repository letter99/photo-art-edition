# Memory Extraction

## Purpose

Find what the photograph leaves in memory, not only what it contains.
Emotional interpretation must be grounded in visible evidence. Do not attach
generic mood words without identifying the light, spacing, posture, color,
weather, rhythm, or omission that creates the feeling.

## Four readings

### Fact

What is physically present: subject identity, spatial layers, positions, scale,
depth and distinctive details.

### Perception

How the eye experiences the frame: visual weight, pace, density, distance,
openness, stillness, movement, tension or pause.

### Memory

What remains after looking away. Express it as a concrete image or sentence,
not only as an adjective.

Example: “The trees seem to rise slowly from still water, as if time has
paused around them.”

### Anchors

What abstraction must preserve: subject identity, core relationship, spatial
hierarchy, visual anchor and emotional direction.

## Abstraction boundary

Abstraction may change exact detail, minor objects, local texture, precise
color, photographic perspective and edge completeness.

It must preserve emotional direction, atmosphere, subject identity, core
spatial relationships and the visual anchor.

It must not invent a new event, contradictory season or weather, new subjects,
or a composition that changes the scene's meaning.
