# Sticker Motif Selection

Stickers are an abstraction of the scene's meaningful parts, not a clipped
thumbnail of the whole Master illustration.

## Selection

Choose a small, scene-supported set whose size is determined by the image, not
by a preset quota. Keep the visual anchor and add only the few other forms
that make the memory legible or give the sheet a useful rhythm. A quiet scene
may need only a few motifs; a dense scene may support more.

Do not invent objects, add generic travel icons, or force a fixed count when
the source cannot support it. A quiet scene may need only three or four strong
motifs; a dense scene may support five or six.

## Abstraction

For each selected motif, preserve identity, silhouette, relative character and
source-derived color relationships, while reducing photographic detail into
the locked illustration medium. A grouped motif may remain one sticker. Avoid
duplicating the same tree, building, person or prop merely to fill space.

## Composition

Arrange motifs as a deliberate sticker sheet with uneven scale hierarchy,
calm spacing, fully visible silhouettes and warm-white die-cut borders. No
overall paper panel, rectangular background, poster typography, cast shadow or
unrelated decoration. The transparent sheet must be independently usable.

## Consistency lock

Record the selected motif names and their relative positions after Master
selection. B may isolate and recompose only those motifs. C may change their
color expression to monochrome but may not add, remove, merge or reinterpret
them.
